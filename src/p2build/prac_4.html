知识讲解两种衰落，两种分集

无线信道的起伏分两个尺度（讲义第 2 章）：

  • 大尺度衰落 = 阴影（shadowing）：楼房、山体挡住传播路径，信号在几十米到几百米的范围内一直偏低，变化慢。它是中断（outage，接收功率低于门限）的主要原因。
  • 小尺度衰落 = 多径（multipath）：多条路径的信号时而相加时而抵消，几个波长内就变化，比如瑞利衰落。

分集（diversity）的基本想法（第 4 章 p.2–3）：把同样的信息经过几条独立的信道送到，一条深衰落时其他的大概率还好。对付哪种衰落，信道之间就要隔多远：

宏分集（macroscopic，p.4）微分集（microscopic，p.5）
对付阴影（大尺度）多径（小尺度）
做法多个基站同时连一部手机，用信号最强的那个同一处多根天线、不同频率、不同时间、极化
间隔基站相距很远（不同发射位置）几个波长就够

为什么同一个基站多装几根天线解决不了阴影：手机被同一栋楼挡住时，几根天线的路径都穿过这栋楼，一起变差。讲义 p.4 原话的意思是：阴影造成的明显起伏只在大的距离上才发生，所以必须是不同位置的基站。

答题要点每一点为什么要写

  1. 先说宏分集是什么：手机的信号同时被几个不同位置的基站接收和处理，选最强的（或合并）。老师说解释题第一步就是描述这个东西是什么。
  2. 中断来自阴影：说明你知道要对付的是哪种衰落。阴影慢而深，靠加功率（加余量）对付很不划算。
  3. 不同位置 → 阴影近似独立：这是机制的核心。路径不同，挡住它们的障碍物不同，一条被挡不影响另一条。
  4. 全部同时中断才算中断 → 概率相乘：独立时 \(P_{out}=\prod_iP_{out,i}\)。这是"为什么能降低"的定量答案。
  5. 条件：链路相关时效果变差（p.21）。写上这一句说明你理解前提，但老师说两点（是什么 + 为什么）就够，多写的部分写错会扣分。

知识讲解相关为什么削弱分集（p.21）

讲义写：P(两条同时深衰落) = P(r₁ 深衰落 | r₂ 深衰落) × P(r₂ 深衰落) ≥ P(r₁ 深衰落) × P(r₂ 深衰落)，链路不相关时取等号。意思是：相关时一条变差，另一条也倾向于变差，"同时中断"比相乘的结果大。例如手机进了地下室，所有基站的信号一起变弱。Practice 2 第 2(c) 题把这件事定量化了：相关系数 0.5 时，上面的 2.5% 变成 6.3%。

易错常见丢分点

  • 只说"有多个基站所以更可靠"，没说独立和全部同时：机制没讲清，拿不到满分。
  • 以为同一基站多天线也行：阴影对它们是一样的。

Concept + walkthrough: why macro-diversity reduces outage

ConceptTwo kinds of fading, two kinds of diversity

Wireless channel variations happen on two scales (lecture Ch 2):

  • Large-scale fading = shadowing: buildings or hills block the path, and the signal stays low over tens to hundreds of metres; it changes slowly. It is the main cause of outage (received power below the threshold).
  • Small-scale fading = multipath: signals on several paths add up and cancel by turns, changing within a few wavelengths, e.g. Rayleigh fading.

The basic idea of diversity (Ch 4 p.2–3): send the same information over several independent channels; when one is in a deep fade the others are probably fine. How far apart the channels must be depends on which fading you fight:

Macroscopic diversity (p.4)Microscopic diversity (p.5)
Fightsshadowing (large-scale)multipath (small-scale)
Howseveral base stations linked to one mobile, the strongest is usedseveral antennas at one place, different frequencies, times, polarisations
Separationbase stations far apart (different transmitter locations)a few wavelengths is enough

Why extra antennas on the same base station do not fix shadowing: when the mobile is behind one building, the paths to all those antennas pass through that building and get worse together. Slide p.4 says, in effect, that the strong variations caused by shadowing happen only over large distances, so the base stations must be at different locations.

Answer pointsWhy each point is needed

  1. First say what macro-diversity is: the mobile's signal is received and processed by several base stations at different locations, and the strongest is used (or they are combined). The lecturer said the first step of an explanation question is to describe the thing.
  2. Outage comes from shadowing: shows you know which fading is being fought. Shadowing is slow and deep, so fighting it with more power (a larger margin) is expensive.
  3. Different locations → nearly independent shadowing: this is the core of the mechanism. Different paths are blocked by different obstacles, so one link being blocked does not affect another.
  4. Outage only if all links fail at once → multiply: with independence \(P_{out}=\prod_iP_{out,i}\). This is the quantitative answer to "why it reduces outage".
  5. Condition: the gain shrinks if the links are correlated (p.21). This sentence shows you understand the assumption, but the lecturer said two points (what + why) are enough, and anything extra that is wrong loses marks.
Single-link outage 10%: two independent base stations 0.1² = 1%, three 0.1³ = 0.1%
With the formula of Practice 2 Q2 and margin Δ = σ: one base station Q(1) = 0.1587, two independent [Q(1)]² = 0.0252

ConceptWhy correlation weakens diversity (p.21)

The slide says: P(both in deep fade) = P(r₁ in deep fade | r₂ in deep fade) × P(r₂ in deep fade) ≥ P(r₁ in deep fade) × P(r₂ in deep fade), with equality when the links are uncorrelated. In words: with correlation, when one link gets worse the other tends to get worse too, so "both in outage" is more likely than the product. For example, when the mobile goes into a basement, the signals from all base stations drop together. Practice 2 Q2(c) makes this quantitative: with correlation coefficient 0.5 the 2.5% above becomes 6.3%.

PitfallsWhere marks are usually lost

  • Saying it fights multipath / Rayleigh fading: that is microscopic diversity.
  • Only saying "more base stations, so more reliable" without independent and all at the same time: the mechanism is missing and full marks are not given.
  • Thinking several antennas on one base station would do: shadowing is the same for all of them.
  • Writing one long paragraph: the lecturer recommends a few bullet points; imperfect English is fine.

知识讲解衰落下要对误码率求平均

AWGN 信道里 SNR 是常数；衰落信道里 SNR γ 是随机变量（老师课上反复强调），所以误码率 Ps(γ) 也是随机的，要求平均：\(\bar P_s=E[P_s(\gamma)]=\sum_kP_s(\gamma_k)\,p_k\)（讲义第 4 章 p.23 的积分式，离散分布就换成求和）。老师说不会考积分，但离散分布的平均误码率会考。

关键：E[f(γ)] ≠ f(E[γ])。本题如果把平均 SNR 10 直接代进去，½e⁻¹⁰ = 2.27 × 10⁻⁵，而真正的平均是 6.15 × 10⁻⁵，差近 3 倍：误码主要来自 SNR 差的那些时刻，平均 SNR 把它们"抹平"了。

知识讲解MRC、阵列增益 g、分集增益 d

  • MRC（最大比合并，p.15–16）：每条支路乘以信道增益的共轭再相加，输出 SNR = 各支路 SNR 之和：γΣ = γ₁ + γ₂。
  • 阵列增益（p.27）\(g=\bar\gamma_\Sigma/\bar\gamma\)：合并后平均 SNR 是输入的几倍。
  • 分集增益（p.27）\(d\approx-\log_{10}\bar P_e/\log_{10}\bar\gamma\)：来自高 SNR 下 \(\bar P_e\approx\bar\gamma^{-d}\)，误码率随 SNR 下降的"斜率"。γ̄ 是合并器输入的平均 SNR。

老师说 g 和 d 的公式"简单、要记住"（蓝框，考试不给）。p.21：支路相关时，同时深衰落的概率更大，分集变差。

详细解析(a) 单支路

  1. γ 就是 α：γ = αPT/σn² 且 PT = σn² = 1 W，所以 γ = 8（1/3）或 11（2/3）。老师提醒先问一句：这些数是线性值还是 dB？这里是线性值，不用换算。
  2. 平均 SNR：Σ 取值 × 概率。
  3. 平均误码率：Σ Ps(取值) × 概率，不能代平均 SNR。
γ̄ = 8 × ⅓ + 11 × ⅔ = 10
P̄s = ⅓ × ½e⁻⁸ + ⅔ × ½e⁻¹¹ = 5.59 × 10⁻⁵ + 0.56 × 10⁻⁵ = 6.15 × 10⁻⁵

Concept + walkthrough: average error rate with discrete SNR, MRC, g and d

ConceptIn fading, the error rate must be averaged

In an AWGN channel the SNR is a constant; in a fading channel the SNR γ is a random variable (the lecturer stressed this repeatedly), so the error rate Ps(γ) is random too and must be averaged: \(\bar P_s=E[P_s(\gamma)]=\sum_kP_s(\gamma_k)\,p_k\) (the integral on lecture Ch 4 p.23 becomes a sum for a discrete distribution). The lecturer said integrals will not be examined, but average error rates for discrete distributions can be.

Key point: E[f(γ)] ≠ f(E[γ]). Substituting the mean SNR 10 directly gives ½e⁻¹⁰ = 2.27 × 10⁻⁵, while the true average is 6.15 × 10⁻⁵, almost 3 times larger: the errors come mainly from the moments of low SNR, and the mean SNR "smooths them away".

ConceptMRC, array gain g, diversity gain d

  • MRC (maximal ratio combining, p.15–16): each branch is multiplied by the conjugate of its channel gain and the branches are added; the output SNR is the sum of the branch SNRs: γΣ = γ₁ + γ₂.
  • Array gain (p.27) \(g=\bar\gamma_\Sigma/\bar\gamma\): how many times larger the average SNR is after combining.
  • Diversity gain (p.27) \(d\approx-\log_{10}\bar P_e/\log_{10}\bar\gamma\): from \(\bar P_e\approx\bar\gamma^{-d}\) at high SNR, the "slope" at which the error rate falls with SNR. γ̄ is the average SNR at the combiner input.

The lecturer called the g and d formulas "simple, remember them" (blue box, not given in the exam). p.21: with correlated branches, simultaneous deep fades are more likely and diversity is weaker.

Beyond the slidesWhy d = 8.1 is larger than the number of branches

Slide p.28 says d ≤ M. Here d = 8.1 > 2 because the d of p.27 is a high-SNR "slope" definition meant for channels such as Rayleigh that have very deep fades (P̄e ∝ γ̄−d). In this discrete distribution γ never drops below 8, so there are no deep fades and the error rate falls exponentially with SNR; evaluating the formula at the single point γ̄ = 10 just gives a number, not a true diversity order (even the single branch of (a) gives 4.2). In the exam, apply the formula as the official answer does and use it to compare (b) and (c).

PitfallsWhere marks are usually lost

  • Substituting the average SNR into the error formula: E[½e−γ] ≠ ½e−E[γ].

  • Using the output 20 in the denominator of d: use the input 10, log₁₀10 = 1.
  • Writing β instead of 2β in the exponent in (c).
  • Keep significant figures for tiny numbers such as e⁻¹⁶; do not round them to 0.

知识讲解 + 详细解析：高斯衰落因子与等权合并

知识讲解判决量是高斯时，误码率 = Q(均值 / 标准差)

本题的接收信号 \(y_k=\alpha_kb\sqrt E+w_k\) 里有两种"噪声"（老师课上的说法）：αk 乘在信号上，是乘性噪声（衰落）；wk 加在信号上，是加性噪声。特别之处是 αk 被建模成高斯（不是瑞利），这样整个判决量都是高斯，误码率可以直接写成 Q 函数。

要用到的三条概率工具（第 1b 章）：

工具式子本题用在哪
常数乘随机变量E[cX] = cE[X]，Var(cX) = c²Var(X)α√E 的方差是 σ₁²E
独立变量相加均值相加，方差相加α√E + w；M 路相加
独立高斯之和仍是高斯z 是高斯，才能写 Q

再加第 2 章 p.14 的"移项"技巧：z = m + n，n ~ N(0, s²)，则 P(z ≤ 0) = P(n ≤ −m) = Q(m/s)。

等权合并（equal weight combining）：所有 yk 直接相加。老师特别提醒它不是讲义 p.19 的等增益合并（EGC）：EGC 要先按各路相位旋转，这里各路都是实数，直接加。

详细解析(a) M = 1：老师总结的三步

  1. 只算 b = +1：b 等概取 ±1，两种情况对称，Pb = ½P(错 | +1) + ½P(错 | −1) = P(错 | +1)。
  2. 求总均值和总方差（老师说这一步最关键）：z = α√E + w。均值 E[α]√E + E[w] = α₀√E；方差 Var(α)E + Var(w) = σ₁²E + σ₂²（α 与 w 独立）。所以 z = α₀√E + n，n ~ N(0, σ₁²E + σ₂²)。
  3. 写成 Q：b = +1 时出错 = z ≤ 0 ⇔ n ≤ −α₀√E，由对称性 P = Q(均值 / 标准差)。
均值 α₀√E，方差 σ₁²E + σ₂²
Pb = Q( α₀√E / √(σ₁²E + σ₂²) )

详细解析(b) M 路等权相加

  1. 写出判决量：\(z=\sum_{k=1}^My_k=b\sqrt E\sum_k\alpha_k+\sum_kw_k\)。
  2. 均值：M 个 α₀√E 相加 = Mα₀√E。
  3. 方差：2M 个独立项，方差相加 = M(σ₁²E + σ₂²)。题目没明说各 αk 相互独立，标准解默认独立（不同天线的衰落独立）。
  4. Q 函数：均值 / 标准差 = Mα₀√E / √(M(σ₁²E + σ₂²))，约掉 √M。
Pb = Q( √M · α₀√E / √(σ₁²E + σ₂²) )

为什么合并有好处：均值按 M 增长，标准差只按 √M 增长，所以 Q 的参数放大 √M 倍。信号是"同向相加"（每路都是 α₀√E），噪声是"随机相加"（有正有负，部分抵消）。

课外补充数值感受与两个极端

举例 α₀ = 1，E = 1，σ₁² = σ₂² = 0.1，参数 = √M × 1/√0.2：
M = 1：Q(2.24) = 0.0127
M = 2：Q(3.16) = 7.8 × 10⁻⁴（蒙特卡洛模拟 8.0 × 10⁻⁴，对得上）
M = 4：Q(4.47) = 3.9 × 10⁻⁶
  • 没有噪声（σ₂ = 0）：误码率 Q(α₀/σ₁) 仍不为 0，因为衰落因子本身可能变成负数，把符号翻过来，这是衰落造成的"平台"；合并后变成 Q(√M α₀/σ₁)，平台降低。
  • 各路 αk 完全相同（完全相关）：方差变成 M²σ₁²E + Mσ₂²，参数随 M 增大最多趋向 α₀/σ₁。上面的例子 M = 4 只有 Q(2.83) = 2.3 × 10⁻³，比独立时的 3.9 × 10⁻⁶ 差得多，又是"相关削弱分集"（p.21）。

易错常见丢分点

  • 方差写成 σ₁² + σ₂²，漏乘 E：α 乘的是 √E，方差要乘 (√E)² = E。
  • 标准差写成 σ₁√E + σ₂：方差能相加，标准差不能。
  • 把等权合并当成 EGC 或 MRC：这里就是直接相加。
  • (b) 只写到 Q(Mα₀√E / √(M(…)))，没约成 √M：不算错，但约完才看得出合并的好处。

Concept + walkthrough: Gaussian fading factor and equal-weight combining

ConceptIf the decision variable is Gaussian, error rate = Q(mean / standard deviation)

The received signal \(y_k=\alpha_kb\sqrt E+w_k\) contains two kinds of "noise" (the lecturer's words): αk multiplies the signal, a multiplicative noise (fading); wk is added to it, an additive noise. What is special is that αk is modelled as Gaussian (not Rayleigh), so the whole decision variable is Gaussian and the error rate can be written directly as a Q function.

Three probability tools are needed (Ch 1b):

ToolFormulaUsed for
constant times a random variableE[cX] = cE[X], Var(cX) = c²Var(X)the variance of α√E is σ₁²E
sum of independent variablesmeans add, variances addα√E + w; the sum over M branches
sum of independent Gaussiansstill Gaussianz is Gaussian, so Q can be used

Plus the "rearrange" trick of Ch 2 p.14: if z = m + n with n ~ N(0, s²), then P(z ≤ 0) = P(n ≤ −m) = Q(m/s).

Equal-weight combining: all yk are simply added. The lecturer specifically warned that this is not the equal gain combining (EGC) of slide p.19: EGC first rotates each branch by its phase, while here all branches are real and are just added.

Step by step(a) M = 1: the lecturer's three steps

  1. Only b = +1 is needed: b = ±1 are equally likely and the two cases are symmetric, Pb = ½P(error | +1) + ½P(error | −1) = P(error | +1).
  2. Find the total mean and total variance (the lecturer said this is the key step): z = α√E + w. Mean E[α]√E + E[w] = α₀√E; variance Var(α)E + Var(w) = σ₁²E + σ₂² (α and w independent). So z = α₀√E + n with n ~ N(0, σ₁²E + σ₂²).
  3. Write it as Q: for b = +1 an error is z ≤ 0 ⇔ n ≤ −α₀√E, and by symmetry P = Q(mean / standard deviation).
mean α₀√E, variance σ₁²E + σ₂²
Pb = Q( α₀√E / √(σ₁²E + σ₂²) )

Step by step(b) Equal-weight sum of M branches

  1. Write the decision variable: \(z=\sum_{k=1}^My_k=b\sqrt E\sum_k\alpha_k+\sum_kw_k\).
  2. Mean: M copies of α₀√E add up to Mα₀√E.
  3. Variance: 2M independent terms, variances add to M(σ₁²E + σ₂²). The question does not state explicitly that the αk are mutually independent; the standard solution assumes so (fading on different antennas is independent).
  4. Q function: mean / standard deviation = Mα₀√E / √(M(σ₁²E + σ₂²)); cancel √M.
Pb = Q( √M · α₀√E / √(σ₁²E + σ₂²) )

Why combining helps: the mean grows like M but the standard deviation only like √M, so the argument of Q grows by √M. The signal adds "coherently" (every branch contributes α₀√E), while the noise adds "randomly" (positive and negative parts partly cancel).

Beyond the slidesNumbers and two extremes

Example α₀ = 1, E = 1, σ₁² = σ₂² = 0.1, argument = √M × 1/√0.2:
M = 1: Q(2.24) = 0.0127
M = 2: Q(3.16) = 7.8 × 10⁻⁴ (Monte Carlo simulation gives 8.0 × 10⁻⁴, consistent)
M = 4: Q(4.47) = 3.9 × 10⁻⁶
  • No noise (σ₂ = 0): the error rate Q(α₀/σ₁) is still not 0, because the fading factor itself can become negative and flip the sign; this is a fading-induced "floor". After combining it becomes Q(√M α₀/σ₁), a lower floor.
  • All αk identical (fully correlated): the variance becomes M²σ₁²E + Mσ₂², and as M grows the argument at most approaches α₀/σ₁. In the example above, M = 4 gives only Q(2.83) = 2.3 × 10⁻³, far worse than 3.9 × 10⁻⁶ in the independent case: again, "correlation weakens diversity" (p.21).

PitfallsWhere marks are usually lost

  • Writing the variance as σ₁² + σ₂² without the factor E: α is multiplied by √E, so its variance is multiplied by (√E)² = E.
  • Writing the standard deviation as σ₁√E + σ₂: variances add, standard deviations do not.
  • Treating equal-weight combining as EGC or MRC: here the branches are simply added.
  • Stopping at Q(Mα₀√E / √(M(…))) in (b) without simplifying to √M: not wrong, but only the simplified form shows the benefit of combining.

◆ Key points
What it tests
BPSK error rate when the fading factor is Gaussian; equal-weight combining (Ch 4 p.19; sums of independent Gaussians, Ch 1b).
Background
A sum of independent Gaussians is Gaussian with means and variances adding; Var(cX) = c²Var(X), so Var(α√E) = σ₁²E.
Method
Assume b = +1 (symmetric) → mean and variance of the decision variable z → error when z ≤ 0 → P = Q(mean / standard deviation).
Key result
Summing M branches with equal weights multiplies the mean by M and the variance by M, so the standard deviation grows by √M and the Q argument by √M.
Common mistakes
Do not forget the factor E in σ₁²E; the standard deviation is √(σ₁²E + σ₂²), not σ₁√E + σ₂.
Physical meaning
Even without noise (σ₂ = 0) the error rate is Q(α₀/σ₁) ≠ 0: the fading factor itself can become negative and flip the symbol, a fading-induced floor. Combining M branches lowers it to Q(√M α₀/σ₁).
Variation
If the receiver knew each αk it could weight by αk (MRC, p.15); here it does not, so equal weights are used.
